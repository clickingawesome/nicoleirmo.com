Back to double digits: the direct channel's first double-digit growth in six years

Ecommerce3 min read2026-07-15By Nicole Irmo

A direct channel that hadn't grown by double digits since 2018 did it within eighteen months. The fix wasn't more traffic. It was what happened after the click.

The direct site had plenty of visitors. What it didn't have was a reason to choose it over a marketplace, or a page that made choosing easy. The product page was a spec sheet. The sample program, which is the single best reason to buy from us directly, was buried. Nobody had rewritten the navigation since the catalog doubled.

This is the most common situation I see in direct-to-consumer brands with a marketplace presence. The marketplace gets the attention because the marketplace has the dashboard. The direct site quietly becomes the place people end up when the marketplace is out of stock.

What we changed

We rebuilt the product page, homepage, navigation and sample program on the new platform. That's the visible work. The invisible work was giving the site a point of view: reassurance first, specs second, for a customer who's often anxious and always short on time.

The product page led with who the product is for and what it will feel like to use, then the numbers. Navigation was rebuilt around the questions people ask, not the categories we use internally. The sample program moved from a footer link to the center of the experience, because "try before you buy" is the argument a marketplace can't make.

+12.5%direct channel growth, year over year, in Q3 2024. The first double-digit quarter since 2018.

The same quarter delivered the biggest sales month in company history at the time. Ten consecutive quarters of growth followed. None of it required more traffic. It required treating the site as the flagship store rather than the overflow lot.

Why "after the click" is the lever

Traffic is expensive and, past a point, it isn't the constraint. Most brands I've worked with could double their conversion rate before they could double their traffic, and doubling conversion costs a fraction of doubling traffic. The lever is what the visitor sees, in what order, and whether the page answers the question they arrived with.

We tested relentlessly, but the tests weren't the strategy. The strategy was the point of view. Tests told us how to express it better. Without the point of view, testing just finds a local maximum on a page nobody loves.

The marketplace question

People ask whether growing the direct channel means neglecting the marketplace. It doesn't. Our Amazon business grew 154% over the same period. The two feed each other: the story we built for the direct site made the marketplace listings better, and the marketplace introduced customers who later came direct for the sample program and the service.

Before buying more visitors, ask what the site says to the ones you already have.

The lesson for any DTC brand

Stand in front of your own product page as a stranger with the problem your product solves. Time how long it takes to feel reassured. If it's more than a few seconds, that's the work. A page that answers the customer's real question is worth more than any campaign pointing at a page that doesn't.
