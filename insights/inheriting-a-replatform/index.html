Inheriting a replatform mid-flight: how to land a project you did not start

Web3 min read2026-06-12By Nicole Irmo

The ecommerce director left in the middle of an enterprise CMS migration, during a promotion, during a vendor change. Here is what kept it on time, on budget and off the incident log.

Nobody plans to take over a replatform halfway through. When it happens, the temptation is to pause everything and re-scope, because the new person naturally wants to own the plan. That is usually the most expensive choice available. A half-migrated site costs you twice: you maintain both and improve neither, and every week of pause is a week your team splits its attention.

I inherited ours mid-flight, in the same month I was promoted and the vendor relationship was changing. There was no clean moment to start. There rarely is.

The first two weeks

We froze the scope to what was already committed and wrote it down, one page, everyone signed. Anything not on the page went to a list for after launch. This felt like giving things up. It was actually the first time the project had a definition of done.

We put one person, me, between the vendor and the business, so decisions stopped bouncing. Before that, the vendor was hearing from four people with four priorities, and a vendor with four masters serves none of them. After, every question had one answer within a day.

We set a launch date that was real, with a rollback plan that was also real. A rollback plan you have actually rehearsed changes the tone of every conversation leading up to launch, because the worst case is known and survivable.

Then we made the platform pay for itself

A migration that just moves the old site onto new software is a very expensive way to change nothing. The point of the platform was what it let us do next. A modular content-block system moved page production in-house: a landing page that used to wait for a vendor sprint now ships the same day. The marketing team went from requesting pages to making them.

The redesigned product page, homepage, navigation and sample program followed on the same foundation. Then Google Pay, Apple Pay, a shipping calculator and UPS Access Point pickup, each one a small removal of friction that would have been a project of its own on the old stack.

What I would tell someone inheriting one tomorrow

Take ownership of the decisions before you take ownership of the backlog. The backlog will be a mess. It always is. Decisions are what you can fix in a week: who signs off, what done means, when it ships, what happens if it fails.

Protect the team from the vendor and the vendor from the team. Both need one voice on the other side. Be that voice, even when it means saying no to people you like.

A replatform is not a technology project. It is an operating-model project that happens to involve technology.

And measure the thing the platform was for. Ours was speed of production. Same-day pages was the number that told the business the migration had been worth it, long after the launch-day adrenaline faded.
