Amazon is a shelf, not a channel: how we grew it 154%

Ecommerce3 min read2026-09-17By Nicole Irmo

We did not buy the growth. We rebuilt the shelf: every listing, every image, every word a shopper reads before deciding.

Brands talk about Amazon as a channel to be managed, like paid search. It behaves more like a shelf in a very crowded store. The shopper is standing in front of it, comparing, and the only thing you control is how your product looks and reads in that moment. Ads can get more people to the shelf. They cannot make the shelf make sense.

Ours looked like a catalog that had grown one product at a time over twenty years, because it had. Titles were inconsistent. Images did not answer the questions shoppers actually have. The story we had built on the direct site was nowhere to be seen. A shopper could not tell our light product from our overnight product without opening three tabs.

$5.7M → $14.4MAmazon revenue, up 154%, with $8.7M added.

Merchandising: make the range read as a line

We rebuilt merchandising so the range read as a considered line rather than a pile. Consistent naming, so a shopper learns the system once. Clear tiers, so "which one" is a two-second decision. Variations grouped the way a person thinks about them, by need, not by SKU history.

This sounds like housekeeping. It moved conversion more than any ad we ran that year, because it removed the moment where a shopper gives up and buys the competitor whose listing they understood.

Content: answer the real questions

We rewrote the content to answer the questions shoppers actually have: how absorbent, for whom, for how long, what happens at night. In hours and measurements, not adjectives. The images were rebuilt to show the same things, because on a shelf the image does most of the reading.

And we brought the brand voice over from the direct site. The same reassurance, the same plain language, the same refusal to hide behind euphemism. A shopper who had seen us anywhere else recognized us here, and recognition is trust.

Why "shelf" is the right word

A channel is something you push through. A shelf is something a person stands in front of. The difference changes what you invest in. Channel thinking buys more traffic. Shelf thinking makes the product easier to choose. Both matter, but only one of them compounds, because a shelf that makes sense keeps converting long after the ad budget is spent.

Ads bring people to the shelf. Only the shelf can make them choose.

If your marketplace numbers are flat

Before you spend on ads, stand in front of your own shelf as a stranger and try to buy the right product for a specific need. Time it. If it takes more than a minute, or if you pick the wrong one, that is the work. Fix the names. Fix the images. Fix the first line of every listing. Then, and only then, buy the traffic. (It is always the work.)
