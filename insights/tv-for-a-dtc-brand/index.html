Why a DTC brand tested television, and what a sub-$3 CPM taught us

Broadcast3 min read2026-06-30By Nicole Irmo

Linear TV and CTV are supposed to be for big brands with big budgets. A $150K pilot said otherwise, and taught us how to buy it without embarrassing ourselves.

The objection to television is always cost. The reality is that connected TV and remnant linear inventory can be bought at CPMs below what most brands pay for social. The harder problem is not the media. It is creative: you need a spot, and a spot needs a story, and a story needs to already exist before the buy.

We had one. The brand had been repositioned around reassurance rather than product features two years earlier, and the words were already on the product page, in the emails and in the customer stories. The spot did not need to invent anything. It needed to say what we already said, to more people.

Owning the creative first

We also had an in-house video producer, hired the year before, which meant the creative cost a fraction of an agency shoot and could be cut into CTV, YouTube and social versions in the same week. This is the part most brands get backwards. They buy the airtime, then discover they need a $200K production to fill it, then decide TV is too expensive.

Build the capability first. A producer on staff who knows the customer will make a better thirty seconds than an agency meeting the brand for the first time, and they will make five more versions by Friday.

How we built the case

The pilot ran alongside paid social, paid YouTube and retargeting that were already returning roughly five times their spend on conversion campaigns. TV did not need to beat that on direct response. It was bought for awareness, and measurement was directional rather than a lift study.

That honesty mattered in the room. We did not promise the CFO a ROAS. We promised a CPM, a creative cost, a reach number and a date. Every one of those was something we could show at the end of the quarter, which meant the second budget conversation was about scale rather than permission.

What it gave a category that rarely advertises was a brand people had seen somewhere before. In a stigmatized category, being seen on a normal screen at a normal hour, sounding normal, is a message in itself.

Buying it without getting burned

Start with CTV, where you can target, cap frequency and see delivery in near real time. Add linear remnant inventory once you know the creative works, because remnant is cheap for a reason and you want to be sure the message survives a smaller, less attentive audience.

Cut the spot for every length you might air, on the same day, from the same shoot. Fifteen, thirty, six. Vertical for social. Square for feeds. The producer who shot it should be the one cutting it, because they know which frame carries the story.

Build the case on CPM and creative cost, not on faith.

If you are considering it

Own the creative capability before you buy the airtime. Run it as a pilot with a number attached, so the second conversation is about scale. And go in knowing what television is for. It is not a conversion channel. It is the reason your conversion channels work a little better the following month, and that is enough. (Yes, TV.)
