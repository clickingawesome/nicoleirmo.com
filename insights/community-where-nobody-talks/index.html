Building community in a category where customers never speak in public

Community3 min read2026-05-28By Nicole Irmo

Nobody posts about incontinence. We built the company's first owned advocacy programs anyway, and engagement rose 144%.

In most categories, community marketing starts with customers who already love you out loud. In ours, the product is private by definition. Nobody tags a brand of adult briefs in a vacation photo. Nobody reviews it with their real name. The usual playbook, find the fans and amplify them, doesn't apply, because the fans are silent.

So the first decision was to stop treating silence as absence. Our customers weren't absent. They were quiet for a good reason, and building community meant respecting the reason while building a room where speaking felt safe.

Building the room before asking anyone to speak

Social media came in-house so the voice was consistent and the responses were fast. When someone does take the risk of commenting in a category like ours, the reply has to come from a person who understands, quickly. An agency on a two-day SLA can't do that. A team member who has read five hundred customer stories can.

We founded a brand ambassador program from scratch, then a creator program alongside it, each giving people a reason and a format to tell their story on their own terms. Some ambassadors show their face. Some show their hands. Some write. The program bends to the person, not the other way around.

And we built the Hero Club, which invited customers and caregivers to share what they wanted to share, with as much or as little of themselves as they chose. Five hundred stories later, it has fed content across the site, email, social and television for more than a year, and every one of those stories reads as real because it is.

+144%engagement year over year; following up from 35,400 to 56,600 across eight platforms.

What the numbers looked like

Monthly follower growth ran at 4.5% against category benchmarks between 0.3% and 1.4%. More importantly, the brand now has advocates in a market where advocacy was assumed to be impossible, and their words feed everything from product pages to television. No-cost user-generated content, in a category where we had been told there would never be any.

What made it work

Respect the privacy, then design around it. Every program has a way to participate without being identifiable. That isn't a compromise. It's the reason people say yes.

Give people a role, not a request. "Would you leave a review" is a request, and in this category the answer is usually no. "Would you be an ambassador for people who are where you were two years ago" is a role, and the answer is surprisingly often yes.

Measure the quiet signals. Saves, shares and direct messages tell you more here than public comments ever will. A post that gets few comments and many saves is doing exactly what it should in a category where people research alone.

Silence isn't absence. It's a room that hasn't been built yet.

Every category has some version of this. A product people need but don't discuss, a decision people make privately, a purchase that carries a little shame it shouldn't carry. The playbook is the same. Build the room. Make it safe. Then wait, because they will come.
