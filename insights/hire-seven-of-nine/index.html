Seven of nine: building a marketing team that stays

Team4 min read2026-08-27By Nicole Irmo

One voluntary departure in three and a half years is not luck. It is the result of a few unglamorous decisions.

I hired seven of the nine people on the team, promoted three of them, and in three and a half years lost one person to another job. In marketing, where turnover is a running joke, that number gets more questions than any revenue figure.

The first decision was to write the plan down. The company had never had a documented marketing plan, so nobody could tell whether they were doing well. Individual strategic initiatives with owned KPIs changed that. People stay where they can see their own scoreboard.

7 of 9hired, 3 promoted, 1 voluntary departure.

The second was to insource the work people wanted to do. Social media and video production had been outsourced. Bringing them in-house gave the team craft to be proud of and gave the company creative that now runs across broadcast, CTV and YouTube.

The third was quarterly strategy reviews that were actually about strategy, not status. Ask people to think and they will surprise you. Ask them to report and they will look for a place where thinking is allowed.

The cost of doing this

It is slower. Documenting, insourcing and reviewing take time you could spend shipping. But a team that stays compounds, and a team that leaves resets you to zero every eighteen months.
