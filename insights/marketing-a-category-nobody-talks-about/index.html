How to market a product nobody wants to talk about

Brand3 min read2026-09-24By Nicole Irmo

Incontinence products are a category people buy quietly and never mention. That is not a marketing problem to work around. It is the whole brief.

Most categories start with a head start: customers who post, review and recommend. Ours starts with silence. People buy the product, hide the box and hope nobody asks. If you run the standard social-proof playbook here, you get very little proof and a lot of awkward asks.

I spent my first months in the category doing what everyone does, which is looking for a way around the stigma. A clever name for the product. A euphemism that would let people talk without saying the word. It did not work, and it should not have, because a brand that cannot say what it sells is not a brand anyone trusts.

The stigma is the story

So we stopped treating the stigma as an obstacle and treated it as the story. The truth is that millions of people manage this every day, at every age, and most of them are managing it well. The hope is a life that does not shrink because of it: the trip taken, the grandchild held, the night slept through. Truth x hope.

Once that was the story, every channel had a job. The product page had to say the true thing plainly and then show the hope. The email had to sound like a person who gets it. The television spot had to put the product on a normal screen at a normal hour and treat it like the ordinary thing it is.

500customer stories captured through the Hero Club, feeding content for more than a year.

Building the room

The Hero Club invited customers to share on their own terms, with as much or as little of themselves as they wanted. Some wrote a paragraph. Some sat for a video. Nobody was asked to perform. Those stories now run through product pages, email, social and even television, and they read as real because they are.

The ambassador and creator programs came next, built from scratch, giving people a role instead of a request. Five hundred stories in, the category that "never talks" turns out to have plenty to say. It just needed somewhere safe to say it.

What this does to the marketing itself

It makes the copy better, because you cannot hide behind adjectives when a customer might read them and know. It makes the creative braver, because the customers were brave first. And it makes the team care differently, because everyone has read the stories. A designer who has read fifty of them does not need a brief on tone.

Say the true thing first, plainly. Then show the hope.

What transfers to any quiet category

Say the true thing first. Build the room before you ask people to speak in it. Give them a role, not a request. And measure the quiet signals, saves, shares and direct messages, because in a category like this, silence and interest look the same from the outside.

If your category has a word people avoid, use it, kindly and often. The avoidance is the stigma doing its work. Saying the word plainly is the first day it stops.
