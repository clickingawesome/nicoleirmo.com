Owned channels pay: why 69% of our web revenue doesn't depend on ad budgets

Growth3 min read2026-07-29By Nicole Irmo

Paid media is rent. Owned channels are equity. Here's why loyalty matters.

Most marketing plans I inherit are upside down. The biggest line is paid media, the smallest is content, and the reporting treats organic traffic as a happy accident. That's a business paying rent on every customer it will ever meet, with a landlord who raises the price every quarter.

I'm not against paid media. I run a lot of it, and I will defend it in any budget meeting. But paid is an amplifier. If you point it at nothing, you amplify nothing, and you pay for the privilege.

Flipping the plan

So we built the other half of the plan. Search, email, community and the blog became the engine, working alongside paid. That's a sentence. It was two years of work. It meant funding content when it wasn't converting yet. It meant building an email program that earned attention instead of demanding it. It meant treating the blog as a store, with every article having a reason to buy and a way to do it.

69%of attributed web revenue from owned channels.

Today, roughly 69% of attributed web revenue comes from channels we own, and organic search alone generates 2.3 times the revenue of paid search. Those two numbers are the argument. Everything else is commentary.

Three moves that did most of the work

First, we treated the blog as a store, not a library. Conversion on blog traffic went from 3.1% to 15.3% once every article had a clear next step and a product that answered the question the reader arrived with. Nothing clever. Just the assumption that a person reading about a problem might want to solve it.

Second, we built authority hubs that answered the questions our buyers actually ask, which is what search engines and AI assistants both reward. The hubs are long, specific and unglamorous. They're also the pages that keep bringing people back after the campaign ends.

Third, we measured owned revenue the way we measured paid. Same dashboards, same attribution rigor, same seat at the table in the quarterly review. When owned channels are measured like a hobby, they get funded like a hobby.

What it changes when the weather turns

When a tariff shock or an ad-platform price hike hits, a rented audience gets expensive overnight. An owned one keeps buying. We've lived through both in the last two years, and the difference in how it felt is hard to overstate. The paid budget had to flex. The owned channels kept working.

Rent goes up. Equity compounds.

How to start if your plan is upside down

Don't cut paid to fund owned. Add owned alongside it, measure both the same way, and let the numbers move the budget over time. Pick one owned channel, probably email or the blog, and give it a real goal for two quarters. Report on it in the same meeting as paid, in the same format. Then watch what the room does when the owned number starts to move.

It takes longer than a media buy. That's the whole argument for doing it first.
