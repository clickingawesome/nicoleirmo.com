Hire for the puzzle: what I look for on a marketing team

Team3 min read2026-09-10By Nicole Irmo

I hired seven of the nine people on my team. The best of them share one trait that never shows up on a resume.

Every marketing hire I have made started with the same question in my head: does this person want to solve the puzzle? Not execute a task. Not own a channel. Put the pieces together, see the whole picture, and then care whether it worked.

It is a strange thing to hire for, because you cannot put it in a job description and you cannot test it with a portfolio. But over twelve years and three teams, it is the trait that has separated the people who compound from the people who are merely competent.

What the puzzle people do differently

Skills matter, and I check them. But a designer who asks what the campaign is for, a writer who reads the sales call notes before writing, a developer who wants to know why the page exists: those people make a team compound. The ones who only want their piece make a team additive at best, and additive teams need managing every single day.

Puzzle people also handle change better, because they are holding the picture rather than the piece. When the plan shifts, they re-place their piece. Task people wait to be told where the piece goes now.

7 of 9hired by me, 3 promoted, 1 voluntary departure in three and a half years.

Making the puzzle visible

Once they are in, the job is to make the whole board visible. Everyone on my team has a written annual strategy with KPIs they own, and everyone from graphics to content to web dev presents their own measured results at the quarterly review. When people can see the whole board, they play differently. When they can only see their square, they play safe.

Consolidating marketing and ecommerce into one team was the same idea at a larger scale. Two teams holding two halves of the picture will always argue about the seam. One team holding the whole thing just fixes it.

Three interview questions that work

Tell me about a time you changed your mind because of a number. Puzzle people have a story. Task people have a shrug.

What did you learn from a campaign that failed? The word "learn" is the tell. Puzzle people talk about what they would do differently. Task people talk about who else was responsible.

Which part of marketing do you wish you understood better? Puzzle people name something adjacent to their role and light up. Task people name nothing, or name something safely far away.

Hire the person who wants to see the whole board. Then show them the board.

The honest caveat

You need some task people. Every team does, and a team of nine puzzle people would spend the week redesigning the puzzle. The mix matters. But the people who lead, who get promoted, who you build the next plan around, are always the ones who could not stop asking what the picture was.
