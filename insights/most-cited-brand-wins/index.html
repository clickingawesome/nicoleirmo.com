In AI search, the most-cited brand wins. Here's how we became it.

Search3 min read2026-09-10By Nicole Irmo

Rankings measure where you show up. Citations measure whether you're the answer. That difference decided our category.

When people started asking AI assistants which product to buy instead of typing the question into a search box, our category had no playbook. Rankings were still being reported every Monday. Citations, the moments when an assistant actually names your brand in its answer, weren't being tracked by anyone.

That gap is the whole story. A ranking is a chance to be seen. A citation is being chosen. In a category where the buyer is often a caregiver, tired, and asking a very specific question at 11pm, being the answer is worth more than being on the list.

1,291citations, against 986 for the nearest competitor. The brand is now the most-cited domain in its category.

What we actually did

First, we put LLM visibility tracking next to the search report and treated it as the number that mattered. That sounds small. It changed every conversation, because the team stopped asking "where do we rank" and started asking "are we the answer, and if not, who is, and why."

Second, we rebuilt the authority content. Not more blog posts. Long-form hubs that answered the questions our buyers actually ask, in the order they ask them, with the specificity an assistant needs in order to quote us. Absorbency in hours, not adjectives. Sizing by measurement, not by "runs small." What to do at night, in plain words.

Third, we wrote for the reader rather than the crawler. This is the part people find hard to believe: assistants reward the same clarity people do. If a human can skim your page and get the answer in ten seconds, a model can too. If a human has to wade through preamble, so does the model, and it will quote someone else.

The three things that made the difference

Moving early. Competitors were still optimizing for a results page when we were already asking what an answer needs to look like. That head start compounds, because once you're the cited source, you tend to stay the cited source.

Structure. Every hub has a clear question at the top, a direct answer beneath it, then the depth. Headings that say what the section is. Tables where a table is honest. Nothing hidden behind a click.

Measurement. Because we counted citations from day one, we could defend the investment when the first quarter looked quiet. Content programs die in the gap between "we published it" and "it worked." A number that moves closes that gap.

Rankings tell you where you stand. Citations tell you whether anyone listened.

What to do this quarter

Pick the ten questions your buyers ask most. Not the ten keywords with the highest volume. The ten questions a real person would ask a knowledgeable friend. Answer each one better than anyone in your category, in plain language, on a page you own.

Then start counting citations. Ask the major assistants your ten questions once a month and record who gets named. It isn't perfect measurement. It's good enough to see a trend, and a trend is what you need to keep the program funded.

If nobody in your company is counting them yet, that's the opening. It was ours.
